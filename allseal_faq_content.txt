Q: What are the differences between PU liquid membrane, cementitious waterproofing, and torch-on membrane?
A: PU liquid membrane is a flexible, seamless coating ideal for bathrooms and balconies because it bonds tightly to tiles and concrete and moves with the building structure. Cementitious waterproofing is a rigid, water-based coating best suited for internal areas like water tanks and basements where flexibility is less critical. Torch-on membrane is a bitumen-based sheet applied with heat, commonly used for flat concrete roofs because it forms a thick, durable barrier, but it requires professional installation and proper ventilation. Allseal Waterproofing selects the right system based on the specific area, substrate, and exposure conditions.

Q: What is the difference between waterproofing and water-repellent paint?
A: Waterproofing is a structural solution that uses flexible membranes or coatings to create a physical barrier preventing water molecules from passing through the substrate, while water-repellent paint is a surface treatment that beads water off but does not stop moisture penetration under hydrostatic pressure. Water-repellent paints may last only 1 to 2 years and are unsuitable for wet areas like bathrooms, roofs, or basements where standing water or high moisture exposure occurs. Allseal Waterproofing recommends genuine waterproofing systems rather than paint for any area that is subject to repeated or prolonged water contact.

Q: When should I use crack injection instead of full membrane waterproofing?
A: Crack injection using PU or epoxy resin is the right solution when the leak is localised to a single, identifiable crack, such as a hairline ceiling crack from an upstairs bathroom or a joint between a wall and floor slab. Full membrane waterproofing is necessary when there is widespread water ingress, multiple crack locations, or a failed existing waterproofing layer covering a large area like a whole bathroom floor or full roof deck. Allseal Waterproofing always conducts a moisture mapping survey first to determine whether a targeted injection or a full membrane system will provide the most cost-effective and lasting solution.
