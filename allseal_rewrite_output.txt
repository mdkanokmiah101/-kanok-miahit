===METHODS===
PU Liquid Membrane|A seamless, high-elasticity waterproof coating ideal for roofs and terraces. Forms a durable, UV-resistant barrier that bonds directly to concrete surfaces for long-term protection.
Cementitious WP|A cement-based waterproofing compound designed for internal wet areas like bathrooms and basements. Easy to apply, it forms a rigid integral waterproof layer within the substrate that resists hydrostatic pressure.
Torch-On Membrane|A traditional bituminous membrane applied by torch for heavy-duty flat roof waterproofing. Provides exceptional durability and puncture resistance against foot traffic, debris, and extreme weather.
PU Injection Grouting|A pressure-injection method using polyurethane resin to seal active leaks in concrete cracks and construction joints. The resin expands on contact with water to create a permanent, flexible waterstop deep inside the substrate.
No-Hacking System|A revolutionary waterproofing technique that applies a high-bond coating directly over existing bathroom tiles. Eliminates dust, noise, and the cost of hacking — the ideal solution for HDB flats and condominiums.
Epoxy Flooring|A high-strength industrial flooring system with built-in waterproofing for basements, carparks, and warehouses. Resists chemical spills, heavy abrasion, and moisture vapour transmission while creating a seamless clean surface.

===WARNINGSIGNS===
Damp Ceiling|Yellowish water stains spreading across your ceiling are a telltale sign of an active leak from the unit above. In Singapore's humid climate, even a small seepage can quickly lead to plaster degradation and mould infestation if left untreated.
Peeling Paint|Bubbling, cracking, or flaking paint on your walls indicates trapped moisture gradually destroying the surface bond. This is especially common in Singapore's older HDB flats where internal waterproofing layers may have deteriorated over time.
Efflorescence|That white, chalky powder appearing on your masonry walls is crystallised mineral salt left behind by evaporating water. It signals moisture actively seeping through your building's concrete or brickwork — a problem that worsens with every monsoon season.
Musty Smell|A persistent damp, earthy odour in any room points to hidden mould colonies feeding on trapped moisture behind walls or under flooring. In Singapore's tropical humidity, this smell is your nose warning you that active waterproofing failure is already underway.
Mould Growth|Black, green, or grey fuzzy patches on your walls or ceilings are a clear indicator of chronic dampness and poor ventilation. Singapore's year-round high humidity accelerates mould spread, which can trigger respiratory issues and damage furnishings within weeks.
Loose Tiles|Hollow-sounding or visibly loose bathroom and kitchen tiles often mean the waterproofing membrane beneath has broken down. When water seeps through failed tile grout into the substrate, it compromises tile adhesion and can lead to slab deterioration over time.
